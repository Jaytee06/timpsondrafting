AI, CAD, THE BUILDING MODEL, AND THE DESIGNER
Prepared for Alma's drafting guide — October 3, 2026

PURPOSE

The goal is a coordinated relationship between concept design, customer
customization, a detailed building model, and construction documentation.
The designer directs that relationship. AI, the viewer, and CAD each provide
different ways to propose, inspect, communicate, and document the same design.

The recommended workflow develops the model and documentation together.
For current projects, established CAD/BIM tools and experienced drafters handle
construction documentation while this viewer develops its modeling and review
capabilities. Model-derived drawings can progressively replace repeated manual
geometry work as the software proves their accuracy.

ONE ACCEPTED DESIGN, MULTIPLE REPRESENTATIONS

The long-term foundation is a semantic building model: objects with identity,
dimensions, relationships, materials, evidence, and design intent. A wall is
more than parallel lines or a solid rendered block. It can contain framing,
voids, insulation, sheathing, barriers, gypsum, cladding, and hosted openings.
A room has boundaries and meaningful dimensions. A window belongs to a wall
and has size, location, and product/specification decisions.

The viewer renders these objects. Plans, elevations, sections, and schedules
are coordinated representations of accepted objects, supplemented by authored
notes, construction details, engineering information, and sheet organization.
Rendered meshes and exported CAD linework should not become competing masters.

Imported DWG/PDF content is source evidence until its meaning is interpreted.
A CAD line might describe a framing face, a finished face, a centerline, a
dimension extension, or unrelated geometry. Source text and geometry must stay
available even when a designer chooses a different proposed arrangement.

THE ROLES

Customer:
Expresses priorities and evaluates spatial, functional, and aesthetic options.
The interface should make choices understandable without requiring CAD skills.

Designer:
Interprets intent, selects meaningful alternatives, resolves ambiguities, and
accepts revisions. The designer decides when a customer conversation should
focus on layout, technical constraints, appearance, or construction details.

AI:
Proposes concepts and scoped changes, explains impacts, and helps find missing
decisions. Proposals carry assumptions and remain distinct from accepted design.
A plausible concept image does not establish measured geometry or a complete
construction specification.

Viewer:
Shows alternatives, physical assemblies, source overlays, dimensions, and
discrepancies. It should support moving walls, resizing openings and rooms, and
comparing finishes while previewing dependent changes before acceptance.

Drafters:
Develop clear, coordinated construction documents, establish drawing standards,
resolve detailing, and provide corrections that improve the model. Drafting
knowledge informs modeling rules as much as the model informs the drawings.

Engineers and other project specialists:
Supply and review the technical decisions within their disciplines. Proposed
framing layouts and generated geometry do not establish engineering adequacy.

THE FEEDBACK LOOP

1. Concept proposal
   Start from a customer brief, an AI-assisted concept, or existing plans.
   Establish dimensions, orientation, levels, and interpreted building objects.
   Mark missing or assumed information explicitly.

2. Interactive design
   Designer and customer compare alternatives in the viewer and drawings.
   A change previews its affected walls, rooms, openings, dimensions, materials,
   and unresolved constraints. Alternatives remain separate from accepted work.

3. Accepted revision
   The designer accepts a defined version. Record the starting revision, affected
   objects, rationale, assumptions, and validation results. Save alternatives
   and allow reversal without losing original source evidence.

4. Technical documentation
   Drafters develop the plan set and specialists supply technical information.
   Generate geometry and views from the accepted model where reliable. Preserve
   authored details, notes, schedules, sheet layouts, and annotations separately.

5. Reconciliation
   A drafting or engineering correction becomes an identified model change or
   review issue. Match by object identity where possible; otherwise interpret
   and review the changed source geometry. Explain conflicts instead of silently
   overwriting another revision or averaging contradictory dimensions.

6. Release
   Review the coordinated package against the applicable jurisdiction and project
   requirements. Preserve the issued revision, model, drawings, specifications,
   and supporting documentation together.

EXAMPLE: ENLARGING A WINDOW

The customer requests a wider window. The designer previews the proposed width
on its host wall. The model checks geometric fit, nearby openings, and affected
room/finish relationships. Framing and finish voids regenerate together. Header
and structural implications remain explicit review items. Once accepted, the
relevant plan, elevation, dimensions, and schedule should update from the same
object. Drafting and engineering feedback may revise the proposal before release.

EXAMPLE: CHANGING A FINISH

A customer compares appearance options. A color or roughness change affects
presentation; panel thickness also affects physical geometry and finished-face
clearances. Each choice should retain its scope, assumptions, and specification
status. Finish options must not silently shift the framing reference or erase
the original assembly recipe. Product selection, coating layers, and construction
performance require more information than a visual appearance label.

HOW ALMA'S DRAFTING GUIDE CAN INFORM BOTH SYSTEMS

For each drafting topic, identify:
  - The design intent and evidence needed to make the decision.
  - The building objects and relationships involved.
  - The drawing conventions and required annotations.
  - The geometric checks and technical review questions.
  - What changes together when an object is edited.
  - What remains authored or specialist-reviewed rather than generated.
  - How acceptance, revisions, conflicts, and issued documents are handled.

For example, a wall-drafting chapter can inform framing/finish references,
assembly layers, opening hosting, wall joins, dimensions, section conventions,
and schedules. A room-planning chapter can inform boundaries, clear dimensions,
circulation, customer options, and the consequences of moving shared walls.
These become modeling requirements and independently measurable test cases.

PERMIT DOCUMENTATION

DWG is an editable delivery format, not evidence that a design is approvable.
The permit package depends on the actual jurisdiction, site, and project.
It can require architectural drawings, structural documents and calculations,
site information, energy documentation, construction details, and other items.
Authority acceptance cannot be promised solely from a rendered model or CAD export.
